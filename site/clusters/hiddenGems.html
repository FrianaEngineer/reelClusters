Hidden Gems

307 films (107 in small clusters, 200 with no actor data)

The periphery bucket: films with no shared-actor connection to any of the ten named clusters above, plus 200 films with no actor credits data at all. Far from uniform -- within it are dozens of small, tightly-connected pockets that are each coherent on their own (usually a single director's filmography or a specific national cinema) but too small individually to place on the hex grid. The analysis below profiles the largest of those pockets.

Small Cluster Analysis (< 10 Films)

19 clusters contain fewer than 10 films. Most are remarkably self-contained — 16 of 19 have a 100% internal ratio, meaning every shared-actor edge stays within the cluster. These clusters tend to organise around a single director's body of work or a tightly defined national cinema.

Cluster Profiles

Cluster 17 — Jia Zhangke (7 films, 17 internal / 0 external, 100%)

The tightest small cluster by internal edge count. Six of seven films are directed by Jia Zhangke (Xiao Wu, Platform, Unknown Pleasures, The World, Ash Is Purest White, Caught by the Tides), with Shadowless Tower (Zhang Lu, 2023) connected via a shared actor. Completely isolated from the rest of the graph — Chinese independent cinema with a recurring cast that appears nowhere else in the Criterion collection.

Cluster 24 — Romanian New Wave (7 films, 10 internal / 2 external, 83.3%)

The only 7-film cluster with external connections. Six films are core Romanian New Wave (The Death of Mr. Lazarescu, 12:08 East of Bucharest, 4 Months 3 Weeks and 2 Days, Police Adjective, Tuesday After Christmas, Sieranevada) joined by Here (Bas Devos, 2023, Belgium). The 2 external edges suggest a small actor overlap with a larger cluster — the weakest boundary of any cluster in this group.

Cluster 27 — West African Cinema (6 films, 6 internal / 0 external, 100%)

Dominated by Ousmane Sembène (Black Girl, Mandabi, Emitaï, Xala, Ceddo) with Touki Bouki (Djibril Diop Mambéty) linked in. A self-contained pocket of Senegalese cinema with no cast overlap to any other Criterion title.

Cluster 35 — New York Underground / Feminist Avant-Garde (6 films, 5 internal / 0 external, 100%)

An unusual thematic grouping: Lionel Rogosin (Black Roots, Black Fantasy), Lizzie Borden (Regrouping, Born in Flames), Lightning Over Water (Wenders & Ray), and Seduction: The Cruel Woman (Treut & Mikesch). Likely connected through documentary subjects and off-Broadway figures who recur across these films rather than professional actors.

Cluster 16 — Makavejev + American Underground (5 films, 5 internal / 0 external, 100%)

Three Dušan Makavejev films (Man Is Not a Bird, Love Affair, WR: Mysteries of the Organism) alongside Burroughs: The Movie and Ornette: Made in America. The connection is likely downtown New York counterculture figures who appeared in multiple films across the Yugoslav and American underground.

Cluster 29 — Harrod Blank / Les Blank (5 films, 7 internal / 0 external, 100%)

A family affair: Les Blank's A Poem Is a Naked Person and four films by or about his son Harrod Blank (Wild Wheels, Oh My God! It's Harrod Blank!, Automorphosis, Why Can't I Be Me?). The high internal edge count (7) for just 5 films reflects dense cast overlap across Harrod's self-referential documentary work.

Cluster 36 — Polish Cinema (5 films, 4 internal / 0 external, 100%)

Wajda's war trilogy (A Generation, Kanal, Ashes and Diamonds) joined by Polanski's Knife in the Water and Smoczyńska's The Lure. The Wajda films share a tight recurring cast; the other two connect in via overlapping Polish actors. Fully isolated from other clusters.

Cluster 8 — Iranian Cinema (5 films, 6 internal / 0 external, 100%)

Kiarostami's Koker trilogy (Where Is the Friend's House?, And Life Goes On, Through the Olive Trees) plus Downpour (Beyzaie) and Chess of the Wind (Aslani). The Koker trilogy's shared cast of non-professional village actors forms the core; the other two films connect via Iranian actor overlap.

Cluster 19 — Mexican / Spanish-Language (4 films, 3 internal / 1 external, 75%)

Two Buñuel films (Él, The Exterminating Angel) plus del Toro's Cronos and Ripstein's Deep Crimson. The single external edge is the weakest boundary here — one shared actor links this cluster to a larger group.

Cluster 31 — Contemporary Italian Cinema (4 films, 6 internal / 0 external, 100%)

Three Paolo Sorrentino films (One Man Up, The Consequences of Love, The Great Beauty) and Garrone's Gomorrah. Dense internal edges (6 for 4 films) reflect Sorrentino's consistent use of the same Naples-based cast, including Toni Servillo who appears across all three Sorrentino entries.

Cluster 39 — Jafar Panahi (4 films, 3 internal / 0 external, 100%)

The White Balloon, The Mirror, No Bears, plus Singh's And Towards Happy Alleys (a documentary partly about Panahi). Completely self-contained Iranian cluster, distinct from Cluster 8's Kiarostami-centred group.

Cluster 15 — Lino Brocka (3 films, 2 internal / 0 external, 100%)

All three films directed by Lino Brocka (Manila in the Claws of Light, Insiang, Bona). Filipino cinema with a recurring cast that has no overlap with any other Criterion title.

Cluster 23 — Music Documentary (3 films, 2 internal / 0 external, 100%)

Monterey Pop, Buena Vista Social Club, and This Ain't No Mouse Music. The shared actors here are likely musicians who appear as subjects across multiple music documentaries rather than professional cast members.

Cluster 28 — Contemporary Queer / Diaspora Cinema (3 films, 3 internal / 0 external, 100%)

Lilting (Hong Khaou), Limonov: The Ballad (Serebrennikov), and Peter Hujar's Day (Ira Sachs, 2025). A thematically coherent but actor-driven grouping of recent films sharing cast across LGBTQ+ and diaspora narratives.

Cluster 38 — Márta Mészáros Diary Trilogy (3 films, 3 internal / 0 external, 100%)

Diary for My Children, Diary for My Lovers, Diary for My Mother and Father — Mészáros's autobiographical trilogy with a fixed cast. Fully self-contained; no Hungarian cast member appears elsewhere in Criterion.

Cluster 46 — Central Asian Cinema (3 films, 2 internal / 0 external, 100%)

Kurosawa's Dersu Uzala (shot in the Soviet Far East) alongside two Kazakh films (Revenge, The Fall of Otrar). The Soviet/Central Asian production context likely accounts for the shared cast.

Cluster 53 — Pedro Costa / Fontainhas (3 films, 3 internal / 0 external, 100%)

Costa's Fontainhas trilogy (Ossos, In Vanda's Room, Colossal Youth), built around non-professional residents of a Lisbon shantytown. The community cast is unique to these films and appears nowhere else in the collection.

Cluster 54 — Bronstein / Baumbach (3 films, 2 internal / 0 external, 100%)

Frownland (Ronald Bronstein), Yeast (Mary Bronstein), and Frances Ha (Noah Baumbach). Ronald Bronstein co-wrote Frances Ha and shares cast with both films; a rare cluster formed by real-world creative relationships rather than national cinema.

Cluster 57 — Lina Rodriguez (3 films, 2 internal / 0 external, 100%)

All three films directed by Lina Rodriguez (Señoritas, This Time Tomorrow, So Much Tenderness). Colombian cinema with a consistent ensemble cast, entirely isolated from the rest of the graph.

Key Findings

Self-containment is near-universal. 16 of 19 clusters (84%) have a 100% internal ratio — no shared actors with any other cluster. This is far higher than the large clusters, which are more likely to have cross-cluster edges.

Director filmographies dominate. Most small clusters centre on a single director's body of work: Jia Zhangke, Sembène, Mészáros, Brocka, Kiarostami, Panahi, Costa, Rodriguez, Wajda, Sorrentino, Harrod Blank. The recurring director–actor relationships produce tightly bonded, isolated subgraphs.

National cinema boundaries hold. Even across directors, clusters correspond closely to national or linguistic production contexts (Romanian, Iranian, Polish, Filipino, Kazakh, Senegalese, Colombian). Actors rarely cross these boundaries within the Criterion collection.

The three exceptions (clusters 24, 19, and 53) each have only 1–2 external edges, suggesting a single actor who happened to appear in a film outside the cluster's primary cultural context — noise rather than structural overlap.
